30-Day Beginner AI Study Plan and Roadmap

A complete course for someone who has just discovered AI. Start from zero, learn the main types of AI, understand what each tool is good and bad at, practise prompting, complete homework every day, and finish with a practical AI workflow portfolio.

Course Goal

By the end of 30 days, you should understand the basic AI landscape, know the difference between chatbots, image generators, video tools, coding assistants, research tools, voice tools, agents, automation tools, and educational AI tools. You will also know how to write better prompts for different outputs: explanations, lesson plans, summaries, images, tables, code, business plans, quizzes, and research notes.

Simple definition: AI means computer systems that can do tasks that usually need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing images, creating text, finding patterns, helping with decisions, and generating media.

How to Study Each Day

  1. Learn: Read the daily notes and watch or complete the linked lesson.
  2. Test: Ask an AI tool to explain the same idea in simple words.
  3. Practise: Use the daily guided prompt.
  4. Compare: Try the same prompt in two tools when possible.
  5. Reflect: Write 5 bullet points: what worked, what failed, what you learned, where AI made a mistake, and how you improved the prompt.

Core Prompt Formula

ROLE + TASK + CONTEXT + AUDIENCE + FORMAT + CONSTRAINTS + EXAMPLES + QUALITY CHECK
Beginner prompt:
Explain artificial intelligence in simple English.

Better prompt:
You are a patient technology teacher. Explain artificial intelligence to a complete beginner in simple English. Use 5 short sections, 3 real-life examples, 5 key vocabulary words, and a short quiz with answers. Avoid technical jargon unless you explain it.

30-Day Roadmap

Day 1 — What Is AI?

Learn: AI is a computer system that performs tasks connected to intelligence: language, images, prediction, planning, and pattern recognition.

Guided practice: Ask an AI: “Explain AI to me like I am 10 years old, then explain it again like I am an adult beginner.”

Homework: Write 10 everyday examples of AI: maps, translation, recommendations, voice assistants, spam filters, etc.

Sample prompt:
You are a beginner-friendly AI teacher. Explain artificial intelligence in simple English with 10 real-life examples and a 5-question quiz.

Day 2 — AI vs Automation vs Software

Learn: Normal software follows fixed instructions. Automation repeats a task. AI can make flexible predictions or generate responses.

Practice: Make a table comparing calculator, email filter, chatbot, YouTube recommendation, and image generator.

Homework: Find 5 apps on your phone and decide whether each uses AI, automation, or normal software.

Day 3 — Traditional AI and Machine Learning

Learn: Machine learning means the system learns patterns from examples instead of being manually programmed for every rule.

Practice: Ask AI to explain classification, prediction, and recommendation using supermarket examples.

Homework: Create 20 rows of fake student data and ask AI what predictions could be made from it.

Day 4 — Generative AI

Learn: Generative AI creates new content: text, images, audio, video, lesson plans, code, summaries, and ideas.

Course link: Complete Google’s Introduction to Generative AI lesson.

Homework: Generate a short explanation, a table, and a quiz about the same topic. Compare the quality.

Day 5 — Large Language Models

Learn: LLMs are AI systems trained to predict and generate language. They do not “know” like humans; they generate likely responses from patterns.

Practice: Ask for the same answer in A1, B1, and C1 English.

Homework: Create 5 prompts that ask for explanations at different levels.

Day 6 — Chatbots

Learn: Chatbots are conversation interfaces connected to AI models. They can tutor, brainstorm, summarize, rewrite, and role-play.

Practice: Use one chatbot as a tutor, one as a writing assistant, and one as a planning assistant.

Homework: Write what each chatbot did well and badly.

Day 7 — Review Week 1

Task: Create a one-page “AI Basics Cheat Sheet.” Include definitions, examples, risks, and 10 useful prompts.

Mini-project: Make a beginner AI glossary with 20 words.

Reflection: What can AI do? What can it not do reliably?

Day 8 — Prompting Basics

Learn: A weak prompt gives vague output. A strong prompt gives role, task, context, audience, format, and constraints.

Practice: Improve this prompt: “Teach me AI.”

Homework: Write 10 weak prompts and improve each one.

Day 9 — Role Prompting

Learn: Role prompting tells AI what expert perspective to use.

Weak: Explain prompts.
Strong: You are a practical AI trainer. Teach prompt writing to a complete beginner using examples, mistakes, and exercises.

Homework: Test roles: teacher, business consultant, programmer, designer, researcher.

Day 10 — Format Prompting

Learn: AI performs better when you specify format: table, checklist, lesson plan, JSON, HTML, email, rubric, worksheet.

Practice: Ask for the same topic as paragraph, table, quiz, lesson plan, and checklist.

Homework: Build a prompt library with 10 output formats.

Day 11 — Constraint Prompting

Learn: Constraints control length, level, tone, vocabulary, audience, and style.

Practice: Ask for “AI explanation for beginners, under 150 words, no jargon, 3 examples.”

Homework: Create 5 prompts with strict constraints.

Day 12 — Iterative Prompting

Learn: Good prompting is a conversation. First output is a draft. Improve it with follow-up instructions.

Practice: Ask AI to create a plan. Then ask: “Make it simpler,” “Add examples,” “Add homework,” “Turn it into a table.”

Homework: Save version 1 and final version. Compare improvements.

Day 13 — Few-Shot Prompting

Learn: Few-shot means giving examples before asking AI to produce similar output.

Example:
Input: beginner
Output: simple words, short sentences, examples.
Input: advanced
Output: technical terms, nuance, citations.
Now create the output for intermediate learners.

Homework: Give AI 2 examples of your preferred style, then ask it to create a third.

Day 14 — Review Week 2

Project: Create your “Prompting Starter Pack”: 25 prompts for learning, writing, research, teaching, creativity, productivity, and coding.

Day 15 — AI for Research

Learn: Research AI can summarize, compare, and organize information, but it must be checked with sources.

Practice: Ask AI to compare three beginner AI courses and explain which is best for a complete beginner.

Homework: Create a source-checking checklist.

Day 16 — AI for Writing

Learn: AI can draft, rewrite, simplify, expand, correct, and change tone.

Practice: Write a paragraph yourself, then ask AI for three improved versions: simple, professional, persuasive.

Homework: Learn to ask for feedback, not just correction.

Day 17 — AI for Images

Learn: Image AI creates visuals from detailed descriptions. Strong prompts include subject, style, composition, background, lighting, color, and text rules.

Image prompt:
Create a clean educational poster showing a friendly robot helping students learn English. Bright classroom, simple icons, modern flat illustration, no text.

Homework: Write 10 image prompts for education, business, and social media.

Day 18 — AI for Voice and Audio

Learn: Voice AI can transcribe speech, generate audio, create listening practice, and support pronunciation learning.

Practice: Record yourself explaining AI for one minute. Use AI to transcribe and improve it.

Homework: Create a 10-line AI conversation script.

Day 19 — AI for Video

Learn: Video AI can help with scripts, scenes, avatars, subtitles, and short clips.

Practice: Create a 30-second video script explaining “What is AI?”

Homework: Turn the script into a shot list with visuals and narration.

Day 20 — AI for Coding

Learn: Coding AI can generate, explain, debug, and improve code. Beginners should ask for small steps and explanations.

Prompt:
Build a simple HTML quiz about AI vocabulary. Use only HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Add comments and explain how to edit the questions.

Homework: Generate a small AI vocabulary quiz webpage.

Day 21 — Review Week 3

Project: Build a “multi-modal AI kit”: one explanation, one image prompt, one audio script, one video script, one small HTML activity.

Day 22 — AI Agents

Learn: Agents can plan and use tools to complete multi-step tasks. They need limits and approval points.

Practice: Ask AI to create a weekly learning plan, but require it to ask before changing anything important.

Homework: Write an agent instruction with goal, tools, limits, and success criteria.

Day 23 — AI Automation

Learn: Automation connects tools. Example: form response → spreadsheet → email → task list.

Practice: Design an automation for student homework tracking.

Homework: Draw a workflow with 5 steps.

Day 24 — AI for Education

Learn: AI can generate lessons, quizzes, rubrics, feedback, reading passages, speaking questions, and differentiated tasks.

Prompt:
Create an A2 ESL lesson about technology and AI. Include vocabulary, grammar, speaking questions, controlled practice, homework, and answer key.

Homework: Create a lesson plan and ask AI to adapt it for A1 and B1.

Day 25 — AI for Business and Productivity

Learn: AI helps with planning, market research, social media, emails, dashboards, customer support, and SOPs.

Practice: Ask AI to create a 7-day productivity workflow for your work or study.

Homework: Build 5 prompts for your real-life tasks.

Day 26 — AI Safety, Bias, and Hallucination

Learn: AI can be wrong, biased, outdated, or incomplete. Never trust important answers without checking.

Practice: Ask AI for sources, then verify them manually.

Homework: Create a “Trust Checklist”: source, date, author, evidence, uncertainty, second opinion.

Day 27 — Data Privacy and Responsible Use

Learn: Do not paste private student data, passwords, legal documents, medical details, or confidential business data into tools unless you understand the privacy terms.

Practice: Rewrite sensitive data into anonymous data.

Homework: Create safe rules for using AI at work or school.

Day 28 — Building Your Personal AI Workflow

Learn: A workflow is a repeated system: collect information, prompt AI, review output, improve it, publish or use it.

Practice: Build a workflow for one real task: lesson creation, study planning, business marketing, or coding.

Homework: Document your workflow step by step.

Day 29 — Capstone Project

Project: Create a complete AI-powered product or learning resource.

  • Option 1: Beginner AI lesson pack
  • Option 2: AI vocabulary quiz webpage
  • Option 3: AI study planner
  • Option 4: AI business workflow
  • Option 5: AI classroom poster + worksheet

Homework: Ask AI to critique your project using a rubric.

Day 30 — Final Review and Next Roadmap

Task: Review everything. Create your next 60-day path: prompting, coding, automation, data analysis, education, design, or AI business.

Final reflection: What will you use AI for weekly? What should you never use AI for without checking?

Final prompt:
You are my AI learning coach. Based on my 30-day AI study notes, create a personalized 60-day advanced roadmap with weekly goals, free resources, projects, and assessment tasks.

Guided Practice Bank

Practice 1: Simplify

Ask AI to explain a hard concept in simple English, then ask for examples and a quiz.

Explain neural networks in simple English. Use an analogy, 5 examples, and 5 quiz questions.

Practice 2: Compare

Ask AI to compare tools or concepts in a table.

Compare ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot for a beginner. Include strengths, weaknesses, and free alternatives.

Practice 3: Create

Ask AI to build something useful.

Create a beginner worksheet about AI vocabulary with answer key and speaking questions.

Practice 4: Improve

Give AI a bad draft and ask for specific feedback.

Improve this paragraph. Make it clearer, more natural, and more professional. Explain every change.

Practice 5: Verify

Ask AI to separate facts, assumptions, and possible errors.

Review this answer. Mark each claim as confirmed, uncertain, or needs checking. Tell me how to verify it.

Practice 6: Transform

Change one output into another format.

Turn these notes into a table, then a lesson plan, then a 10-question quiz.

Sample Prompt Outputs

Prompt 1

You are an AI teacher. Explain generative AI to a complete beginner in 120 words with 3 examples.

Expected Output Style

Generative AI is a type of artificial intelligence that creates new content. It can write text, make images, produce music, generate code, or help design lessons. For example, you can ask it to write an email, create a picture of a classroom, or build a simple quiz. It is useful because it saves time and gives ideas quickly. However, it can make mistakes, invent facts, or misunderstand your request. You should always check important information. A good prompt tells the AI exactly what you want, who the audience is, and what format you need.

Prompt 2

Create a table of 10 AI tools for beginners. Include purpose, free option, paid option, and best use case.

Expected Output Style

A clear comparison table with tool categories, not just brand names. Good answers should mention chatbots, research tools, image tools, coding assistants, automation platforms, note-taking tools, and voice tools.

Prompt 3

Act as a strict editor. Check this AI-generated lesson plan for factual problems, unclear instructions, missing steps, and weak activities. Then rewrite it.

Expected Output Style

A useful answer should first diagnose problems, then provide a stronger revised version. This is better than asking AI only to “make it better.”

Final Notes

  • AI is a helper, not a replacement for judgment.
  • Better prompts usually produce better outputs.
  • Always check important facts, legal information, medical information, academic sources, and personal data rules.
  • The best way to learn AI is by building small useful projects every day.
  • Your final goal is not to memorize tools. Your final goal is to understand workflows.
